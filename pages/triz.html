What is TRIZ?

A premier disruptive technology for innovation, derived from empirical data — the patent record of how inventive people solved inventive problems.

A systematic process for innovation

TRIZ is a systematic process that develops critical thinking skills and promotes creativity and innovation. Elements of TRIZ can be effectively used by a wide range of people — from children to adults — and throughout many industries and sciences.

TRIZ (pronounced TREEZ) is the Russian acronym for the Theory of Inventive Problem Solving. This proven algorithmic approach to solving technical problems began in 1946 when the Russian engineer and scientist Genrikh Altshuller studied thousands of patents and noticed certain patterns. From these patterns he discovered that the evolution of a technical system is not a random process, but is governed by certain objective laws. These laws can be used to consciously develop a system along its path of technical evolution — by determining and implementing innovations.

One result of Altshuller's theory — that inventiveness and creativity can be learned — has fundamentally altered the psychological model of creativity.

TRIZ developed in the Soviet Union, moving underground after Altshuller was imprisoned for his "heretical" work, until the fall of the USSR when TRIZ re-emerged and migrated to the West. Today many Fortune 500 companies successfully use TRIZ methodology, and its use has expanded into bio-medical research, medicine, computer programming and business management.

TRIZ does not compete with or replace other quality methods. It synergistically complements methodologies like QFD, Six Sigma, Kaizen and Lean, and can be used to address their weaknesses.

"The evolution of successful technological systems is not random, but is governed by certain laws or prevailing trends."

The founder

Genrikh Altshuller, 1926–1998

Genrikh Altshuller grew up in the remarkable city of Baku, Azerbaijan, in the former USSR. Baku was a cultural ecotone where European, Islamic and Asian cultures met. It is probably significant that TRIZ, which encompasses both Eastern and Western thought modalities, originated within such a diverse east-west climate. At age fourteen, Altshuller was awarded his first Soviet patent for an underwater breathing device.

Over one dozen patented inventions — and one engineering degree — later, Altshuller found himself working as a consultant in the Caspian Sea Naval Patent Office. He searched for a simple, scientific way of classifying inventions by their degree of inventiveness. After correlating thousands of patents he discovered that there were only five distinct levels of inventiveness, and several of these did not represent actual "inventions" but merely alterations or extensions of existing ones.

His analysis revealed that all technical systems evolve along natural and predictable lines of evolution, and that all industries utilized the same underlying innovative principles. Therefore these principles could be generalized for universal application — and innovation could be learned. It could be taught.

Even when Stalin rewarded Altshuller with imprisonment in a Siberian labor camp, he continued his work. He formed a "University for One" where persecuted intellectuals, authors, and college professors taught him their subjects of expertise. After Stalin's death, Altshuller was released. Unable to openly teach TRIZ, he began writing science fiction under the pen name Altov. He continued developing and teaching TRIZ with dozens of co-investigators throughout his life, eventually applying it to non-technical fields.

The Altshuller Institute is the only organization officially authorized by Genrikh Altshuller to use his name. More on Altshuller — an interview, his last communiqué, notes on his teaching, and tributes — is collected in the Library.

Why TRIZ works

Fundamental factors that point to TRIZ as an effective and transformational methodology

  • It is very general.
  • It is grounded in a reliable body of knowledge, the growing patent base.
  • The scenarios it generates bypass the psychological inertia of team members.
  • It can be used with other quality processes, removing roadblocks and speeding up decision-making.
  • It reduces the resources required to generate a solution set.
  • It is rapid in generating solution concepts, relative to other methods.
  • It can be taught, and applied, at any level of education, from school children to management consultants to PhD scientists.

Standards

The TRIZ Body of Knowledge

In order to elevate TRIZ to a larger stage, the development of a viable certification program is required. The Altshuller Institute spearheaded an initiative to define a universal set of guidelines so that TRIZ terms have the same meaning regardless of where the TRIZ user was trained.

The International TRIZ Association (MATRIZ), the Altshuller Institute for TRIZ Studies and the European TRIZ Association (ETRIA) mutually agreed to define the TRIZ Body of Knowledge. It was developed jointly by Simon Litvin, Vladimir Petrov and Mikhail Rubin of MATRIZ and Victor Fey, the Institute's Education Director.

To be included, a TRIZ concept, component or tool must be used by the majority of TRIZ schools in the world, in the majority of non-commercial publications by TRIZ Masters, and in the majority of TRIZ courses presently offered.

Structure of the Body of Knowledge

  1. Foundational concepts — dialectics, technological system, functions, ideality, sufields, IFR, contradictions, system operator
  2. Trends (laws) and sub-trends of technological system evolution
  3. ARIZ — Algorithm for Inventive Problem Solving
  4. Substance-Field analysis and the system of 76 standards
  5. Techniques for resolving contradictions — 40 principles, Contradiction Matrix, separation principles
  6. Scientific effects — physical, chemical, geometrical
  7. System analysis methods — function analysis, trimming, cause-effect analysis, failure anticipation

Recognition

TRIZ Masters

Genrikh Altshuller personally appointed the first TRIZ Masters before his death on September 24, 1998 — more than sixty practitioners from Baku, Saint Petersburg, Moscow, Kishinev, Novosibirsk and beyond, many of whom later brought TRIZ to the United States, Israel and Europe. Since October 14, 2006, the MATRIZ Dissertation Board has appointed new TRIZ Masters.
